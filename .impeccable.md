## Design Context

### Users
Kids aged 10-14, checking their own progress after football training or matches. They open Golazo on their phone (or parent's phone) to see XP earned, log what they did, and track streaks. Sessions are short (1-3 min), usually right after training or before bed. Self-motivated but need the app to maintain that motivation loop.

### Brand Personality
**Encouraging, addictive, colorful** — Duolingo energy applied to football development. Three words: *rewarding, streaky, alive*. The app should make kids feel like every training session counts toward something bigger. Streak anxiety is a feature, not a bug.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Light theme, vivid accents** — colors pop on white, feels energetic and approachable
- **Team colors override everything** — the player's club identity takes priority over app branding
- **Numbers are heroes** — XP, level, streak days, goals scored dominate the visual hierarchy
- **Motion is feedback** — every tap, every level-up, every streak milestone should feel physical
- **Reference apps**: Duolingo (streak/progress loop), Techne Football (training logging), FIFA Ultimate Team (player cards, rewards)
- **Anti-references**: Corporate dashboards, dark-theme fitness trackers, generic sports news apps

### Design Principles
1. **Progress is visible** — The dashboard exists to answer "am I leveling up?" within 2 seconds
2. **Celebrations earn attention** — Level-ups and achievements are the emotional peaks. Go big.
3. **Simplicity for young users** — No dense data tables. Big tap targets. Emoji as icons.
4. **Identity through team** — The player's club colors make the app feel "theirs"
5. **Streak creates habit** — The 🔥 streak counter is the most important retention mechanic
